The note toolbox

1. A figure set by hand

The second note of the first group (La 5) is set to a negra. It leaves its beam group, so Si-b 5 before it prints its own flag and Sol 5 Fa 5 stay one group. Nothing moves: the spacing follows the time, not the figures.

2. Fingers on single notes and on a chord

Finger 4 over La 5 (one number above a note of the upper staff), and 1 and 4 on the chord Sol 4 Re 5 at about 0:25: a column of numbers left of the chord, one at the height of each note.

3. A beam group cut in two

The group of four corcheas is cut in front of its third note: two groups of two.

4. Two notes beamed by hand

Si-b 5 and La 5 are beamed by hand as one group of their own: the group of four becomes two groups of two (Sol 5 Fa 5 stay together). Pressed again on the same two notes, the beam goes away.

5. The grace note switch: on

La 5 is written as a grace note of Sol 5 (a small slashed note just before it), although the rule wrote it as a corchea.

6. The grace note switch: off

The small Fa 5 before Do 5 (a grace note by the rule) is written as a normal note: a semicorchea (its own length) with its own column, so the line takes a little more room.

7. A trill over three onsets

Si-b 5, La 5 and Sol 5 become one held note (a blanca: its length now runs to Fa 5) with tr over it. The two onsets inside the trill stay in the notes and still play; they are not drawn.

8. A mordent and a turn

Two onsets written as a mordent (Si-b 5 and La 5: the mordent over Si-b 5), then a turn over Fa 5 and the notes after it in the hand (Sol 5, Fa 5).

9. A rolled chord and a fermata

The chord Sol 4 Re 5 is rolled (the wavy line left of it); the negra Do 5 after the grace note gets a fermata.

The range toolbox

10. 8va over the right hand

The four high corcheas and the negra after them are written an octave lower under one 8va line: the ledger lines of Si-b 5 and La 5 go away.

11. 8vb under the left hand, in the bass clef

The low notes of the left hand (Mi 2 and lower) under an 8vb line, written an octave higher than they sound: their ledger lines below the staff go away. Phase 7 allows an 8vb in the bass clef (the clef rules forbid only 8va there).

12. The treble clef for the left hand over a stretch

The left hand plays high here (La 3 and above). It reads the treble clef from the start of the stretch and goes back to the bass clef after it (a small clef at each change).

13. An armadura for a stretch

One bemol for the stretch: a becuadro cancels Mi-b and the new armadura keeps Si-b only, on both staves. No note of the stretch is a Mi-b, so no note needs a sign (a Mi-b would print its bemol, the user's rule 1). After the stretch the two bemoles of the piece come back.
