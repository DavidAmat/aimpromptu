Gallery of patterns

Implementation 04-3, Phase 2: the patterns of each hand for the levels of the Whole Song simplification, found in Chopin (snapshots of your PDFs) or drawn by the app (the easy patterns of the brief, and the variations of easy, medium and hard asked in your review). Each pattern has a number inside its hand and level (Hard 3), to name it in the Enhance with AI dialog. Every Chopin passage is also written in the app's notation from the kern of the first edition and drawn by the app, with the snapshot of the edition under it; the app writes one voice per hand, no tuplets, and its own accidentals and clefs. The drawn patterns share one melody in Fa major (two bars of Fa, one of Do, then Sib and Do), so that only the left hand changes from one to the next; the drawn right hands use the same harmony over the held triad of the left hand. The right-hand patterns of review 3 come from a study of every two bars of 198 works: they show the app's drawing of the kern of the first edition, without a snapshot. In each snapshot the other hand is grey. The names are the ones the level prompts of Phase 3 will use.

Easy 1 Slow melody in single notesslow-melody-single-notes

Si4 held for three beats, then Do5: the melody alone, with long notes and no chords. Easy because the hand has one note at a time and almost no movement.

Easy 2 Melody in single notesmelody-single-notes

Fa#5-Sol5-Fa#5-Do#5-Re5-Si4 in corcheas, then La#4: one line of single notes inside one hand position. Easy because there are no chords, also when the notes are corcheas.

Easy 3 Chords in one positionchords-in-one-position

Two- and three-note chords with the melody on top, all inside one octava, while the left hand runs. Easy because the hand does not move, although every note is a chord.

Easy 4 Repeated note with chordsrepeated-note-with-chords

One note repeated in corcheas, with a chord below it on the strong beats (the repeated note of Prelude 15, now in the right hand). Easy because the hand stays on the same keys.

Easy 5 Repeated two-note chordrepeated-two-note-chord

The same third played again in negras, with one note changing now and then. Easy because two fingers stay on two keys.

Easy 6 Five-finger melodyfive-finger-melody

The melody inside five keys (Mi4 to Do5) in negras and blancas. Easy because each finger has its key and the hand never moves.

Easy 7 Chords on each beatchords-on-each-beat

The triad of the harmony on every negra (Fa4-La4-Do5, Mi4-Sol4-Do5, Fa4-Sib4-Re5). Easy because the chord changes only with the harmony and stays in one place.

Easy 8 Repeated chord in corcheasrepeated-chord-in-corcheas

The same triad played again in corcheas. Easy because the hand keeps one shape; only the speed asks something.

Easy 9 Two-note chords in one positiontwo-note-chords-in-one-position

Fa4-La4, then La4-Do5, in negras: two notes of the triad, then the two above them. Easy because the hand does not move.

Easy 10 Broken triad in one positionbroken-triad-in-one-position

Fa4-Do5-La4-Do5 in corcheas (an Alberti figure in the right hand). Easy because the three fingers stay on the three keys of the chord.

Medium 1 A second note on long notessecond-note-on-long-notes

Single notes on the short figures, and a second note (a third, a sixth) only on the long ones; three notes only to land on a long note. Medium because the hand stays fluent and the chords come when there is time for them.

Medium 2 Melody in chords, one rhythmmelody-in-chords-one-rhythm

The melody on top of three-note chords on every negra, with the same rhythm (corchea with puntillo, semicorchea, two negras) every two bars. Medium because every note is a chord, but the hand stays in one place, the rhythm repeats and the tempo is slow (it was hard before review 3).

Medium 3 Thirds and sixths in corcheasthirds-and-sixths-in-corcheas

Two notes on every corchea (Fa5-Reb5, Mib5-Sol4) after a long two-note chord. Medium because the notes are fast but the hand repeats one shape in one place (it was hard before review 3).

Medium 4 Full chords in octavesfull-chords-in-octaves

Chords of four and five notes, ff, with the melody doubled at the octava and a jump up of an octava (La4-Do#5-La5, then La5-Do#6-La6), in negras and blancas. Medium because the chords are slow and are the usual chords of Mi and La with one or two altered notes; the jump is the only difficulty (it was super hard before review 4).

Medium 5 Broken chord over two octavasbroken-chord-over-two-octaves

Fa5-La5-Do6-Fa6-La6, then down: the notes of one chord in corcheas, going up two octavas and coming back. Medium because the hand must move, but it follows the same chord all the way.

Medium 6 A trill in thirdstrill-in-thirds

Two thirds that rock in semicorcheas (Sol#-Si, then La-Do#), again and again. Medium at a moderate tempo because the hand never moves; Chopin asks for it very fast, which makes it an étude.

Medium 7 Two-note chords in a short-long rhythmtwo-note-chords-dotted-rhythm

Thirds and sixths with a short note before a long one, the same figure every beat, with a few flats outside the armadura. Medium because of the rhythm and the alterations; the hand stays in one place.

Medium 8 Mazurka rhythmmazurka-rhythm

A short note, then a long one on the same key, repeated, with a decoration note at the start. Medium because the rhythm is uneven, although the notes repeat.

Medium 9 Broken chord that keeps its shapebroken-chord-shape-moving

A broken chord in semicorcheas that is played again one step higher, with the same shape. Medium because it is fast and moves, but the fingers repeat one figure.

Medium 10 Two-note chords climbingtwo-note-chords-climbing

A melody in thirds and sixths in corcheas that climbs slowly, one note of the chord changing at a time. Medium because the hand moves up with a regular shape.

Medium 11 Melody with a decoration notemelody-with-a-decoration-note

Long notes, each with one decoration note before it, and a few accidentals. Medium because of the decoration notes; the hand stays almost still.

Medium 12 Quick run into a notequick-run-into-a-note

A few fast decoration notes that run up into a two-note chord, then a calm melody. Medium because the run is short and lands in the position of the melody.

Medium 13 Melody in thirdsmelody-in-thirds

Every note of the melody with the third under it, also on the corcheas. Medium because two fingers move together along the melody.

Medium 14 Melody landing on a chordmelody-landing-on-a-chord

Single notes, and at the end of each phrase a chord of three notes of the harmony under the melody note. Medium because the chord comes only on the long, last notes.

Medium 15 Broken chord up and down in semicorcheasbroken-chord-up-and-down-in-semicorcheas

Fa4-La4-Do5-Fa5-Do5-La4-Fa4-La4 at every half bar. Medium because it is fast and covers an octava, but the figure never changes.

Medium 16 Turn around the melodyturn-around-the-melody

Each negra of the melody played as four semicorcheas around it (the note, the step above, the note, the step below). Medium because it is fast, but it is the same turn every time.

Medium 17 Chromatic neighbourchromatic-neighbour

Each negra of the melody comes from the semitone below (Sol#4-La4, Si4-Do5). Medium because of the small alterations, one per beat, in one position.

Hard 1 Melody with small pieces of chordsmelody-with-pieces-of-chords

The melody on top (Sib5, Lab5, Solb5) with a second voice and two-note pieces of chords under it, and a few decoration notes. Hard because the hand plays the melody and part of the harmony (the brief's example of Ballade No. 4).

Hard 2 Melody over an inner linemelody-over-inner-line

The melody in negras with puntillo on the fifth finger, and a line of corcheas under it in the same hand. Hard because one hand plays two voices with different rhythms.

Hard 3 Runs in groups of 7 and 8runs-in-groups

Runs of semicorcheas in groups of 7 and 8 over the left hand's sweeps. Hard because they are fast, but each group is mostly a chromatic scale with a small jump, which the hand plays with one fingering (it was super hard before review 4).

Hard 4 Scale runscale-run

A fast scale over two octavas or more, with the alterations of its key only. Hard because it is fast and the hand moves, but not super hard: a scale keeps one pattern and one fingering (the user's rule of review 4; it was medium before).

Hard 5 Melody over a chromatic inner linemelody-over-chromatic-inner-line

Two-note chords on top and, under them in the same hand, a line of semicorcheas that moves by semitones. Hard because one hand plays two voices fast, with many accidentals.

Hard 6 Thirds climbing by semitonesthirds-climbing-by-semitones

Thirds in corcheas that climb chromatically, with naturals and flats on almost every chord. Hard because the shape is regular but every chord is altered.

Hard 7 Chords moving in a runchords-moving-in-a-run

Three-note chords in corcheas that go down one after the other, under an 8va line. Hard because the hand moves with full chords in the same run (the user's hard case).

Hard 8 Fast run without a fixed shapefast-run-without-a-shape

A run of fusas and shorter notes that goes up and down with no repeated figure, after a decoration note. Hard because the fingers cannot repeat a shape.

Hard 9 Wide arpeggios with accidentalswide-chromatic-arpeggios

Arpeggios in fusas over two octavas, with sharps outside the armadura; the shape repeats on each chord. Hard because the notes are very fast and the hand crosses a wide range.

Hard 10 Double notes climbing in a dotted rhythmdouble-notes-dotted-climbing

Two-note chords with a short note before a long one, climbing, with decoration notes and accidentals. Hard because the double notes move up while the rhythm stays uneven.

Hard 11 Double notes that jumpdouble-notes-that-jump

Two-note chords in corcheas that jump between positions, one after the other. Hard because each chord needs a new hand position.

Hard 12 Double notes in semicorcheasdouble-notes-in-semicorcheas

Thirds and sixths in semicorcheas with naturals and flats, doubled by the left hand. Hard because the double notes are fast and change their alteration often.

Hard 13 Fast melody with many accidentalsfast-melody-many-accidentals

A melody in semicorcheas with a few double notes, and naturals on many notes. Hard because the line is fast and keeps leaving the armadura.

Hard 14 Fast figure around a notefast-figure-around-a-note

Semicorcheas that turn around one note with chromatic neighbours (a double sharp among them), against a left hand in another rhythm. Hard because of the speed and the alterations, although the figure repeats.

Hard 15 Chords climbing in corcheaschords-climbing-in-corcheas

Hard 16 Sixths in semicorcheassixths-in-semicorcheas

Sixths that go down by steps and back in semicorcheas. Hard because the hand plays double notes fast while it moves.

Hard 17 Melody in octavasmelody-in-octaves

The melody doubled at the octava below. Hard because the hand stays open on the octava and jumps with every note.

Hard 18 Chromatic runchromatic-run

Eight semitones up from the top of the chord and back, in semicorcheas, with a sharp on every other note. Hard because it is fast and every note is altered.

Hard 19 Trill on long notestrill-on-long-notes

The melody with a trill on its blanca. Hard because the trill asks for fast, even fingers while the melody goes on.

Super hard 2 Full chords that jumpfull-chords-jumping

Super hard 3 Double notes that change shapechanging-double-notes-with-ornaments

Super hard 4 Cadenza runcadenza-run

A run of very short notes down from Si6, outside the beat (a cadenza), which ends in two-note chords played at the same speed: Sol#4-Re#5 (a fifth), La#4-Do#5 (a third), Sol#4-La#4 (a second). Super hard because of those chords: their spacing changes from one chord to the next, so the hand cannot keep one shape, inside a fast run that goes down. The range is not the reason: the run itself uses only the notes of Si major, and a fast scale of the key over many octavas is easy to hard.

Super hard 5 Very fast wide zig-zagwide-zigzag-very-fast

Super hard 7 Octavas in semicorcheasoctaves-in-semicorcheas

Super hard 8 Chords jumping an octavachords-jumping-an-octave

Super hard 9 Chromatic thirdschromatic-thirds
