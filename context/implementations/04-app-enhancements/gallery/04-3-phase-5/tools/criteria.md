# What a good answer is (the user's criteria for the levels)

The task: a piano sheet that came from a machine transcription of a recording (often impossible to play: chords of
more than an octava, octavas the recording does not have, a left hand that jumps on every note, a melody hidden among
chords and decoration notes) is rewritten at a level (easy, medium, hard; or one level per hand). Each answer below
rewrote one passage of a song. Notes are written in the F1 frames text: one line per column, `f<frame>` (a frame is
40 ms), then the notes of the right hand (`R`) and of the left hand (`L`) with their figures. The answers are shown as
the app draws them (the app chooses the beams, clefs, 8va and decoration notes by its own rules).

## The principle

Difficulty is a matter of hand movement, of keeping patterns, and of how many notes each hand plays. Corcheas are not
hard when they always follow one pattern: a pianist who knows the chord knows which notes to play. Easy and medium must
keep one pattern through the passage, so that the pianist can read it at first sight.

## The levels

| Level | Right hand | Left hand (left-hand companion) |
|---|---|---|
| Easy | The main melody in single notes, no chords. Every melody note kept; a short one may be simplified, never dropped. Accompaniment chords that the transcription put in the right hand move to the left hand, or are dropped when the left hand already plays chords | Simple chords: triads (Fa-La-Do, Fa-Lab-Do), symmetric. A low note and then the chord is allowed (Fa2, then Fa3-La3-Do4). Chords may move in steps with the melody. No hard chords such as Do-Mi-Sol-Sib. The hand jumps at most from a low note to its chord; after that it stays in one position |
| Medium | Single notes in fast passages (corcheas, semicorcheas). On negras, blancas and redondas, two notes (Do3-La3, Do3-Mi3, Do3-Fa3; no octavas); three notes only to land on a long note | As easy, plus rhythms and patterns as in Chopin's Waltzes: a low note then two chords; the same chord repeated; the same chord with one note changing; octavas in the bass; a broken chord in corcheas after a low note. One pattern kept through most of the passage |
| Hard | The melody with small pieces of chords that add to it (Ballade No. 4); where the right hand plays a riff, the fifth or the third of the chord under each note for power | The medium patterns with movement (the second chord moves; irregular lines in corcheas); once the hand is in a position it makes no big jumps; symmetric up and down figures |

## Rules for every level (the user's reviews)

1. **The melody comes first**: every note of the right hand's top line stays at its time (or the same name an octava
   lower or higher), unless it is an artifact. A shortened melody note keeps its onset.
2. **Never harder than the sheet now**: where a hand already plays single notes or one small chord again and again, it
   keeps its notes and only gets a regular, symmetric rhythm. A level never adds a waltz or an arpeggio where the hand
   did not play one.
3. **The new left hand follows the rhythm the left hand plays now**, simplified (the same main onsets), instead of a
   new rhythm from the catalog.
4. **The riff**: where the right hand plays only artifacts or nothing, and the line of the highest notes of the
   left-hand chords is a melody (a riff, an intro), that line moves to the right hand, every note of it.
5. **Artifacts** of the transcription may go, for example a very high chord far above the melody that adds nothing.
   An artifact is left out, never kept an octava or two lower.
6. **Decoration notes** only where it is very clear that a note is an ornament; easy and medium have none.
7. **Easy and medium keep a steady rhythm**: no figure shorter than a semicorchea, no uneven pairs of notes; a melody
   note may move to the nearest corchea or semicorchea of the beat.
8. **The harmony of the recording is kept**: the left-hand companion changes the way the chord is played, not the
   chord; the lowest left-hand note at each change of chord is the bass or the root of the chord.
9. **The patterns asked**: when the run names patterns (the user chose them in the catalog), the answer uses them
   where they fit, and the declared lines (`# pattern ...`) say which one each stretch uses.
10. A hand that moves less is easier: moves per negra (a "move" is a change of the hand's position, as a pianist's
    open hand of one octava) should go down, and the widest column and the range inside two negras should fit the
    level (easy left hand: at most 3 notes within 9 semitones; medium: 3 within 12; hard: 4 within 12).
