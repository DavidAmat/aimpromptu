# P4.3 — does a 40 ms grid change who plays what?

Task: P4.3 `[AITU]`. Decisions implemented: D-31; the question P1.9 left open.
Date: 2026-08-07. Status: done, measured.

## 1. The doubt this task exists to settle

The hand splitter decides, for every onset, whether the left or the right hand played it. It does
that by minimising an ergonomic cost expressed in semitones and **seconds**: how far a hand has to
travel, whether it had time to get there, whether a span is physically possible.

It was written and tuned against a semifusa grid, which at 178 beats per minute is about 21 ms a
column. It now runs on 40 ms columns. Worse, it does not read the recorded onset times at all: it
decodes the matrix and multiplies column indices by the column length, so every onset it sees
carries up to half a frame of error. At 40 ms that is ±20 ms per note.

P1.9 found that the splitter ran on the new grid without modification and said so, and left the
quality question to this task. That is the question: **does the error reach the answer?**

## 2. Method

Measurement rather than argument. The same playing is split at several frame lengths and the two
assignments are compared note by note. A splitter that is really reading the music gives the same
answer on every grid fine enough to hold that music.

Comparing them needs care, because the columns are exactly what changes. Each note is therefore
keyed by its pitch and its **own recorded time in seconds**, taken from the build report, not by its
column. A column's group time will not do either: a chord takes the earliest onset in it, and which
notes share a column also moves with the frame length.

Two subjects: the real *Mr Blue Sky* recording, 2751 recorded notes over 310 seconds, and a written
passage held in `tests/test_hands_on_wall_clock.py` so the result is pinned rather than remembered.

## 3. Results

On the real recording, splitting at 40 ms and comparing against three other grids:

| Grid | Columns | Onsets placed | Notes whose hand changed | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 40 ms | 7760 | 2669 | — | 1.2 s |
| 20 ms | 15519 | 2672 | **5 of 2662 (0.2 %)** | 1.3 s |
| 10 ms | 31037 | 2672 | 6 of 2667 (0.2 %) | 1.6 s |
| 80 ms | 3880 | **2371** | 40 of 2305 (1.7 %) | 1.4 s |

Two things to read from that.

**Between 10 and 40 ms the answer does not move.** Five notes out of 2662 change hand when the grid
is made twice as fine, which is two tenths of one per cent, and making it four times as fine adds
one more. The snapping error is not deciding anything. 40 ms is safe for the hand split, and the
decision to keep it needs no defence beyond this table.

**At 80 ms the grid starts eating the music.** Three hundred onsets disappear, not because the
splitter got worse but because notes shorter than one frame are refused outright (D-05), so the
splitter is handed less music than was played. The hand changes on 1.7 % of what is left. This is
worth stating because it is the argument against ever raising the default: the failure is upstream
of the splitter and it is silent.

Runtime is flat. Halving the frame length doubles the columns and costs about 8 %, which matches
what `hands/beam.py` claims about itself: its cost tracks polyphony, not resolution.

## 4. The audit of `hands/`

Every number in `hands/config.py` is in semitones or seconds, and none is expressed in columns, so
nothing there had to change. One constant touches the grid:

`min_available_time = 0.02` — how long a hand must have before it counts as free to move. It is
20 ms, and the shortest gap a 40 ms grid can express is 40 ms, so the floor never binds. The two
numbers are one halving apart, which is close enough to be worth a test rather than a comment; if
either moves towards the other, `test_the_splitters_time_floor_sits_below_the_frame_length` fails
first.

`hands/events.py` is where the snapped times enter, in `decode_matrix`: `onset_time = onset_col *
time_step`. That is the source of the ±20 ms, and section 3 says it does not matter. It could be
removed, because `TimeBuildReport.event_seconds` already holds every note's recorded time, but a
change that fixes nothing measurable is not worth the risk to a component with a tuned cost model.
Recorded here as an option, not taken.

## 5. Things I believed that turned out false

I expected the fast section of the piece to be where the disagreements were, because 40 ms is a
sixth of a 115 ms gap and that is where PRD §7 says the model is least precise. The five
disagreements are not concentrated there. The reason, in hindsight, is that the cost model asks
whether a hand *could* have travelled in the time available, and a 20 ms error on that question only
matters when the answer is near the boundary, which is rarer than a short gap is.

I also expected to have to weaken the comparison to make it pass, keying on columns and accepting a
tolerance. That was unnecessary once the notes were keyed by their own recorded times, which are in
the build report for exactly this kind of question.

## 6. Tests

`tests/test_hands_on_wall_clock.py`, five tests, all passing:

- **the split is identical at 40 ms and at 20 ms**, and every one of the 48 notes survives both
  grids, so the comparison is not quietly narrowing.
- the same holds at 10 ms.
- **the hands are the ones a player would use**: the held low octaves are the left hand while the
  melody dips below middle C and stays in the right. A stable wrong answer would pass the first two
  tests, so this one checks the content.
- a frame longer than the notes takes them away from the splitter: a 60 ms note survives 40 ms and
  is gone at 80 ms.
- the splitter's time floor sits below the frame length.

Whole backend suite: **601 tests, 5 skipped, no failures.** `flake8` clean; `mypy` reports the same
six baseline errors, none in a file this task touched.

## 7. What the next task inherits

Nothing is blocked. `hands/` needed no change, so P4.4 can rename storage paths without touching it.

The open option from section 4 — feeding the splitter the recorded times instead of the column times
— should be reconsidered only if a real piece produces a visibly wrong split, and the table in
section 3 is the baseline it would have to beat.

`frameMs = 40` can stop being described as provisional for this purpose. The checklist's open
question about it concerns how fast passages *read*, which is P8.2, not whether the hands are right.
