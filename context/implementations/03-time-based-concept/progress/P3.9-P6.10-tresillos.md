# P3.9 and P6.10 — tresillos, and four things the demo piece found

Tasks: P3.9 `[AITU]` and P6.10 `[GN]`, both added during the task. Decisions implemented: D-32 (new).
Date: 2026-08-07. Status: done, checked on screen.

Building a demo piece hard enough to be worth looking at — two hands that never line up, and one of
every figure the vocabulary has — turned up four bugs that no test had. They are in §9. That is the
argument for the demo script existing at all: a real recording tells you the page looks plausible,
and a piece whose every onset was placed on purpose tells you whether it is right.

## 1. The problem

The vocabulary is a ladder of halves. Every figure is double or half its neighbour, which is what
makes the proportional comparison in D-11 clean — and which means there is no name in it for three
even notes filling one beat.

At a negra of 300 ms three such notes land 100 ms apart. A corchea is 150 and a semicorchea 75, so
100 is a poor match for both, and D-11 will pick one of them and print it. Three notes the player
placed evenly then come out as a ragged mix of corcheas and semicorcheas — the same failure this
whole refactor exists to remove, one level down.

Rounding cannot fix this. There is no figure to round to. The group has to be recognised.

## 2. The rule, and why it is narrow

A wrong tresillo is worse than a missed one. A missed one prints a rhythm that is slightly off; a
wrong one tells the reader the piece is in triplets when it is not.

So two conditions, both required:

- **The three gaps match each other**, within 12 %. This is what says the player was dividing a beat
  into three, rather than playing something else at a similar speed. Twelve per cent is roughly the
  jitter a steady player shows.
- **That gap is a third of a figure the ladder already knows**, within 15 %.

Three gaps, not two. Three notes have two gaps between them; the third gap is the one carrying the
last note to whatever comes next, and it is what says the group is even all the way through rather
than three notes followed by a pause.

Groups do not overlap. Six even notes are two tresillos, not four: a note belongs to one group or to
none. Dotted figures cannot be divided, because a dotted figure already divides into three and a
triplet of one would be a second way of writing the same rhythm.

Nothing new appears in the vocabulary. The three notes take the ordinary figure one step below the
one they divide — a tresillo of a negra is three corcheas — and the number 3 with its bracket is what
makes it a tresillo. Their `fitError` is zero, because they are exactly what the mark says they are;
reporting 33 % would be reporting an error the page does not contain.

## 3. On the peak plot

A pile of gaps at a third of a negra is now labelled *corchea de tresillo* instead of *corchea, 33 %
off*, because "33 % off" tells the user the measurement is bad when it is in fact precise.

Only the negra is offered this way, and only within 8 %. Two thirds of a negra is also a third of a
blanca, and the long half of a swung pair lands almost exactly there — on Mr Blue Sky at 212 ms. The
first version of this labelled the shuffle a tresillo and broke three existing tests, which is how
the narrowing was found rather than reasoned.

## 5. Decisions taken inside the task

**Tresillos are found before any figure is chosen.** The whole point is that the nearest figure is
the wrong answer, so asking for it first and correcting afterwards would mean two places that decide
what a note is called.

**They are found per passage, not per hand.** "A third of a negra" is a different number of
milliseconds in each passage, and a group must not be judged against a ladder that does not apply
where it sits.

**Each hand gets its own range of tuplet ids**, threaded through as `tuplet_start_id`. Both hands
starting at 0 made the renderer treat a left-hand tresillo and a right-hand one as the same group.

**A beam is cut at every tuplet boundary.** Two tresillos in a row would otherwise beam as one run of
six and read as a sextuplet. The bracket is only drawn when every member of a beam belongs to the
same tuplet, so the two rules cannot disagree.

**The bracket goes on the beam side**, with the numeral in a gap left between two strokes and the
hooks turning back towards the beam. That is where a reader looks for it, and the noteheads are what
the bracket is describing.

## 6. Things I believed that turned out false

I believed the hooks should point away from the beam, which is what I drew first. They point towards
it: a bracket closes around what it describes.

I believed "about a third of a negra" was close enough as a peak-plot rule. It is not — see §3.

I wrote a test asserting that 100 ms at a negra of 600 is not a tresillo. It is one: at that ladder
the corchea is 300 and 100 divides it exactly. The test became a three-way check instead — 300 → a
tresillo of the negra, 600 → of the corchea, 450 → of nothing, because the vocabulary has no figure
there.

## 8. Checked on screen

`scripts/make_demo_pieces.py` writes two pieces into the store, so the review is a piece picked from
the audio library rather than a recording someone has to find. *Classical mix: tresillos, runs and
holds* has the left hand playing tresillos throughout and the right hand entering half a beat late,
so the two hands never line up and the left-hand notes fall between the right-hand ones — the case
David asked for. The right hand plays negras, a blanca, four beamed corcheas, a tresillo, a dotted
negra, a beam mixing corcheas and semicorcheas with a second beam level, a fusa run with three
levels, two more tresillos and a redonda to finish on. *Even and swung* is the plot's own test: a
straight run and then the same notes shuffled.

The beat is 480 ms rather than something brisker, because a fusa is an eighth of it. At a negra of
300 the fusas land 37 ms apart, under the default 40 ms column, and the grouping rule quite correctly
reads them as chords — two notes that close together were played together. At 480 a fusa is 60 ms and
the run reads as a run at the resolution the app opens on.

Naming the 480 ms pile a negra, the plot labels all seven piles at 0 % off — 60 fusa, 120
semicorchea, 160 *corchea de tresillo*, 240 corchea, 480 negra, 720 *negra con puntillo*, 960 blanca —
and the staff draws 25 tuplet marks across 28 beam groups, with no rests and no console errors.

## 9. Four bugs the demo piece found

**A hand was measuring the other hand's attack.** Grouping runs before the hand split (D-31), so a
column's recorded time is the earliest onset in it whichever hand played it. In the demo the left
hand's tresillo lands 20 ms before one of the right hand's fusas; they share a column at 40 ms, and
the right-hand note inherited the left hand's time. Its gap to the next note came out 40 ms instead
of 60 and the next one 80, so an even run of eight printed as *fusa, fusa, semifusa, fusa…* — the
ragged mix this refactor exists to remove, produced by the refactor itself. `TimeBuildReport` now
records the raw time per key as well as per column, and each hand measures from its own attack. Two
tests in `test_time_pipeline.py` cover it, including one asserting the two hands still share the
column: the correction is to the measured time, not to where anything is drawn.

**The renderer's vocabulary stopped at the sixteenth.** The backend's ladder goes down to the
semifusa, and `VEXFLOW_FIGURE` was quietly mapping both fusa and semifusa to `sixteenth` — a page
saying the piece was played half as fast as it was. `thirtysecond` and `sixtyfourth` are now figures
in the package, with their Bravura flag glyphs and metrics, and the flag lookup is a table indexed by
beam level rather than a two-branch conditional.

**Rest glyphs were being drawn.** D-16 says there are none: distance on this page is time, so a
silence is already the space it takes and the dashed lines crossing it. The score view now passes
`rests: false`, which the package gained as an option.

**A piece could not end on a tresillo.** The rule needs three even gaps, and the last note of a hand
has no gap to a next onset — its third "gap" is the run-out to the end. In the demo that made the
closing three left-hand notes print as two semicorcheas and a negra among sixty-three corcheas. The
final group is now judged on its two inner gaps, with the tail required only not to be markedly
shorter.

**The walk was promising something that stopped being true.** "Renaming a figure moves nothing" was
true before beams existed, and P6.7's tighter spacing inside a beamed run broke it: the width of a
column now depends on the printed figure, so renaming a passage shifts the notes inside it. That is
worth having — a beam that reads as loose notes is not a beam — but it had to be written down rather
than left as a surprise. It is now **D-33**, with the important half of the promise re-stated and
pinned: nothing *outside* the renamed passage moves, which is what D-21 and success criterion 2 are
actually about. `vexflow-v2/tests/ladder-locality.test.ts` asserts both halves.

A fifth thing was not a bug but a bad failure mode. The D-07 guard refuses gaps that are all exact
multiples of the column length, because that is the signature of measuring on snapped columns — and
also of a MIDI file, which is exact because a machine played it. The peaks route returned a 500 for
one. It now measures anyway and returns the plot with a plain-language warning attached, so a real
D-07 regression is loud in the UI instead of being a stack trace, and an exact piece is drawn.

## 10. What is left

Beam slant across a tuplet is the package's existing rule and was not touched. Quintuplets and
other tuplet counts are not implemented; `TupletMark.count` is already a number rather than a
constant 3, so the renderer needs nothing, but the backend only looks for threes.
