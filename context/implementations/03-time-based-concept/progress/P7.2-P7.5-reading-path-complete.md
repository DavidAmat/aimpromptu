## 3. Decisions taken inside the task

**Silence compression needed no code.** The renderer already measures every column from what is
drawn in it, so a column where nothing starts collapses to a sliver. Passing `pixelsPerFrame` turns
that off, and the first version of the score view passed it. Removing one line made a four-second
pause take a small space and put a fourteen-second piece on one line. Distance on the page now reads
as how much is happening, which is what D-22 and D-26 ask for.

**Overrides are applied in the browser, not sent back for a rebuild.** An override is one glyph
drawn differently, so asking the backend to rebuild the score for it would be a round trip that
cannot change anything else anyway. They are merged into the figure lookup the renderer reads.

**An override belongs to a chord, not a notehead.** A click arrives as `hand:frame:row`, and the
override is keyed `hand:frame`. Two notes struck together are one event, and giving one of them a
different figure would print a chord that disagrees with itself.

**A speed change is a click and a number, not a dialog.** The reader clicks the column numbers where
the piece changes and types what a gap is worth from there. The frames come straight from the
renderer's own range selection, so the boundary is a place on the page rather than a number to look
up.

**Playback plays the recording.** Not a reconstruction from the grid. A sheet is a reading of a
performance, so the honest thing to hear is the performance, and it makes the page checkable by ear.

## 4. Things I believed that turned out false

I expected P5.2 to be the largest piece of renderer work left. It was already built and I had
switched it off. Worth remembering before writing anything new in that package: content-measured
column widths are the default there, and passing a fixed width is the special case.

I also expected the per-note override to need the backend to store it. It does not, for the reason
in §3. Storing it belongs to whatever saves a score, not to drawing one.

## 5. Checked on screen

Each of these was run in a browser and read off the page, not inferred:

- A four-second pause between two phrases collapses to a small gap, and a fourteen-second piece
  fits on one system.
- Clicking a notehead and choosing a different figure changes **one** glyph. Every notehead x
  position is byte-identical before and after. That is D-17.
- Adding a speed change from column 144 renames the second half from `negra = 337 ms · ≈178 BPM` to
  `negra = 674 ms · ≈89 BPM`, and again every note x position is byte-identical. That is D-21.
- Pressing play sounds the recording and the elapsed time advances against the written length.
