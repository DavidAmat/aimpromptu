## The three that changed how the model works

Seven of the ten are interface. Three are not, and they are the ones to remember.

### P8.5 — a hand correction belongs to the recording

The hand used to be an overlay: a set of keys held beside the matrix and folded into a copy of it
in the browser. That is wrong, and the reason is D-14. A note's printed length is the gap to the
next onset **in the same hand**, so moving one note between hands renames three things: its old
neighbour, which now runs on to a later onset; its new neighbour, which is cut short; and itself,
which takes its length from where it landed.

Measured on *Mr Blue Sky*: **twenty-five sampled single-note moves each renamed at least one other
note, five on average.** `negra` → `dottedNegra`, `blanca` → `redonda`, tresillos breaking up. The
overlay moved the notehead and left every one of those figures alone, so every one was drawn wrong.

The correction now goes upstream of everything: `PUT /time/{id}/hands` writes the hand onto the
note event in `events.json`, and the matrix is *built* corrected. Figures, beams, clefs and octave
brackets fall out of the ordinary path with nothing left that could disagree. Stored against the
raw time the key went down, not a column, so it survives a change of time resolution.

Taking a note **off the page** stayed an overlay, deliberately: that one is a statement about this
page, and the recording is still evidence that the key went down.

With it came a cache of the hand split, keyed on (piece, column length, recording mtime). The split
is the expensive half of drawing a sheet — a second on a five-minute piece against fifty
milliseconds for the printed notes — and nothing the reader edits changes it. Redraw after an edit
went from **1.1 s to 0.07 s**, which is what makes redrawing on every correction bearable at all.

### P8.6 — a real search bug, found by looking at the page

David pointed at one moment: the left hand up at Fa-5 under six ledger lines above the bass staff,
the right hand down at Fa-2 under six below the treble, for three onsets, then the two swapping
back. Nothing in the music asks for that.

Two independent causes, and the first was a bug in the hand-split search. When no candidate
partition is reachable from any surviving hand position — which the pedalled sustains of a real
transcription produce **53 times on this one file** — the search relaxes the group rather than
dropping it, but it wrote its result unconditionally. Two states differing only in *history*
collapse to the same key, so whichever was enumerated last won, regardless of cost. One unplayable
chord therefore handed the eleven onsets before it to a path 15 units worse. Cheapest wins now.
**Total cost over the piece: 660.6 → 433.2.** The fallback also warns for itself; only the rarer
kind of failure had been warning before.

The second cause was that the split had no idea what its assignments cost the page. It is charged
for the ledger lines now.

### P8.9 — the automation that existed to hide a bug

The screen used to seed itself with octave brackets on every rebuild. On *Mr Blue Sky* that was
**twenty brackets nobody asked for**. They existed for a reason — a bracket is how you get a
passage stranded far outside its own staff back inside it — and P8.6 removed the reason. Once the
split stops stranding passages, an automatic bracket is more often a bracket over music that did
not need one, which is worse than none: the reader has to notice it and clear it before the page
reads plainly.

Nothing proposes them now. They start empty, load from a saved reading, and change only when the
reader clicks the Octave pill. `suggestOttavas` is untouched in the package — exported, tested, and
with no caller.

## Things believed that turned out false

1. **"The hand is a display choice."** It is an input to every figure on the page. Twenty-five
   sampled moves, five collateral renames each. Anything derived from the split has to be rebuilt
   from a corrected split, not patched afterwards.
2. **"A helpful default is free."** The octave suggestions were a default with a cost the user pays
   every time — twenty things to notice and clear. A suggestion that is usually wrong is worse than
   silence.
3. **"An override is a piece of state."** It is a statement *about* something drawn. When the thing
   it is about leaves the page, the statement has to stop being drawn and stop being saved — but
   not be deleted, because the notes can come back and the reader never withdrew what they said.
4. **"The screen's layout is the page's layout."** Three systems to an A4 sheet against the five a
   printed score carries. The missing two were furniture that only means something on screen: the
   frame numbers, the repeated title, the slack under the lower staff.
5. **"Renaming forty chords must move something."** It does not. A column is a slice of wall clock
   and a figure is a label, so a passage rename leaves every onset and every column exactly where it
   was. This is the payoff of the whole refactor, and P8.10 is the first place it was leaned on
   hard.

## What this says about the next plan

Every one of the ten came from watching the app draw a real piece, and two of them (P8.5, P8.6)
were defects that no test in the suite could have found, because the suite tests the pieces we
built to have known answers. The next plan should be written the same way round: play real music
first, collect what is wrong, then plan.
