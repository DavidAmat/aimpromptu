# P7.9 — a line on the staves showing where the recording is

Task: P7.9 `[AITU]`. Decisions implemented: D-01, D-29.
Date: 2026-08-08. Status: done, checked on screen.

## 1. Why this was needed

The Rhythm screen could already play the recording under the sheet, which is what makes the page
checkable by ear: if the notes and the sound drift apart, the page is wrong. But the only way to
tell *where* in the piece the sound was is a clock reading in the transport, and a five-minute piece
is thousands of columns across a dozen systems. A reader listening for one wrong bar had no way to
find it.

So the sheet needed to say where the recording is, on the page, while it plays.

## 2. What a person can now do

Open **Rhythm**, name a gap, press **Play the recording**. A line runs down both staves at the
moment the recording is at, and the page scrolls when the music moves to the next system.

The bar above the sheet is also clickable now. Clicking it moves the recording to that point and the
line with it, which is how you look at one particular passage without waiting for the piece to reach
it.

## 3. What was built

Almost nothing, because the drawing package already had the hard part. `placeCursor` has been in
`@aimpromptu/grid-notation` since the editor was built: give it the last render and a **fractional**
column, and it returns the x, the top and the height of the line, on the right system. The
interpolation inside a column is what matters and is easy to get wrong: column widths follow content
rather than duration, so the line's speed across the page is not constant, and interpolating between
the two edges is what makes it reach column *n* at exactly *n × frameMs* whatever that column's
width.

Three small pieces were added around it.

**Seconds become a fractional column by dividing.** `frames = seconds * 1000 / frameMs`. No lookup
and no clock object, because a column is a fixed slice of wall clock (D-01). This is the whole
conversion.

**The position is read on every animation frame, from the audio element.** The `timeupdate` event
fires about four times a second, which is fine for a clock reading and far too coarse for a line
moving across a page: it would jump about eight staff positions at a time. A timer of our own was
refused for a different reason — it would drift against the sound within a minute, and then the page
would be lying about where the recording is. The audio element stays the only source of truth
(D-29).

**The line is moved, not redrawn.** It is an absolutely positioned element over the score, and a
tick sets three CSS values. Re-rendering the score sixty times a second to move one line would
rebuild every note sixty times and cost more than the rest of the page put together.

## 4. Decisions taken inside the task

**The page scrolls only when the system changes.** Scrolling on every tick fights a reader who has
scrolled somewhere deliberately. Following the music onto the next line is worth interrupting for;
following it across a line is not.

**`play()` is awaited and its failure is shown.** This was found while checking the feature and is a
real bug rather than a detail: the old code called `void element.play()` and set the button to
"Pause" whatever happened. A browser refuses playback it did not see a click for, and a file that
failed to load rejects too, so the transport could sit reading "Pause" over a recording that was not
playing and had no way to say so. It now stays on "Play" and prints the reason.

**Clicking the progress bar seeks.** Added because the feature could not otherwise be checked
without real playback, and kept because it is independently useful: it is the only way to jump to a
passage. It sets `currentTime` on the same audio element, so the sound and the line cannot disagree.

## 5. Things I believed that turned out false

I expected to have to compute the line's position from the note x values, and to add something to
the renderer to expose them. Neither was necessary: `placeCursor` was already written, already
exported and already tested in `tests/player.test.ts`, including the two cases that matter — that it
interpolates inside a column and that it follows the music onto the next line. Reading the package's
own exports before writing anything saved the whole task.

I also believed the playhead would be verifiable by pressing Play in an automated browser. It is
not: a synthetic click is not a user gesture as far as the autoplay policy is concerned, so the
recording never started and the line sat at zero looking broken. That is what led to the two changes
in section 4, and the seek is what finally made the behaviour observable.

## 6. Files

Changed, in `aitu-frontend`:

- `src/components/time/TimeScoreView.tsx` — the `playheadSeconds` prop, the marker and its placement
- `src/components/time/ScorePlayer.tsx` — the `onTime` callback on an animation frame, the seek, and
  the play failure
- `src/pages/playground/RhythmPage.tsx` — holds the position and passes it between the two

Nothing changed in `aitu-backend` or in `vexflow-v2`.

## 7. Tests

No automated test was added. What this task contributes is a DOM element positioned from a function
that already has its own tests in the package (`tests/player.test.ts`, "the playhead" describe
block, two cases). A frontend test would either repeat those against a mock or need a real browser
with a real audio file, and neither would have caught the thing that actually went wrong here, which
was the autoplay refusal.

`tsc -b` and `npx eslint src` are both clean.

## 8. Checked on screen

Read off the page, not inferred:

- With the sheet written and nothing playing, no line is drawn.
- Clicking the progress bar about three quarters along scrolls the page to the system starting near
  column 5738, which is 229 seconds at 40 ms a column, and draws the line inside it. The transport
  and the line agree.
- Clicking a different point moves the line to a different system.
- The line crosses both staves of the system it is on, and no other.
- No console errors.

**What to check yourself.** Open the Rhythm tab with *MR BLUE* loaded, name the 337 ms gap a negra,
and press **Play the recording**. A blue line should run along the staves in time with the sound, and
the page should scroll when the music reaches the next system. Then click part-way along the bar
under the transport: the recording and the line should both jump there.

## 9. What is left

The line follows the recording. It does not yet let you click a note to hear from there, which is the
other half of the same idea and belongs with P7.10, where a score gains state worth saving.
