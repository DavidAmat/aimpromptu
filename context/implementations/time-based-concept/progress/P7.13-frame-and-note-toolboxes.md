# 1. P7.13 — picking a stretch of columns, picking a set of notes, and two toolboxes

Task: **P7.13, new.** Not in `plan.md`. Decisions: none new; it restores a way of working the old
Music Notation tab had.

## 1.1 Why this was needed

P7.12 found that the key signature had been lost with the retired Music Notation tab and that no
phase was tracking it. This task is the rest of that loss. That tab had two toolboxes, one for
columns and one for notes, and the gesture that filled them: pick a group of columns, hold Shift and
pick another, and everything between them is selected.

The Rhythm tab had one column gesture, a drag over the ruler, and it fed one control. A notehead
could be clicked, but only the first of them was kept, so nothing could be done to a set of notes at
once. And a passage could not be given its own key, which is the operation David named: a piece
often changes key part-way through, and writing the whole of it in one signature puts the
accidentals back beside the noteheads exactly where choosing a key was meant to remove them.

## 1.2 What was built

**Picking a stretch of columns.** In the drawing package, the ruler's clickable groups now report
whether Shift was held, and the renderer extends the selection instead of replacing it. A plain
click starts again. Every group inside the stretch is outlined, not only the two that were clicked,
which is what makes the selection readable at a glance.

**The two toolboxes.** `aitu-frontend/src/components/common/ToolboxDialog.tsx` is a floating panel
dragged by its title bar. It is not a modal: the reason it is open is that the reader wants to look
at the sheet while they use it, and a modal would cover the page and block it. It stays inside the
window, and it opens at its default corner each time rather than where it was dragged to three
selections ago.

The **Frames** toolbox opens on a column selection and names the stretch in both columns and
minutes. It carries one operation for now: give this passage its own key signature, or send it back
to the piece's key. The **Notes** toolbox opens on a note selection and names the set. It carries no
operations yet, by David's instruction: it is the panel the controls will live in.

**A passage with its own key.** The drawing package has drawn a key transition since long before
this refactor, and it draws exactly what David described: naturals cancelling what the outgoing
signature printed, both clefs again, then the incoming signature. Giving a passage a key writes two
transitions, one at each end, so the piece returns to its own key afterwards. `aitu-backend`'s
`SavedRhythm` gains `keyChanges`, a list of `{fromColumn, keySignature}`, beside the piece's own key.

**Letting go of a selection.** Escape drops both selections and closes both panels. Closing a panel
drops its own selection, because a dashed outline left behind says something is still picked when
nothing is, and the next click would then extend an invisible selection instead of starting a new
one. Clicking another group without Shift replaces the selection, which it already did.

## 1.3 Decisions taken inside the task

The key signature for a passage is set from the toolbox, and the key for the whole piece stays in
its own control beside the sheet. They are different decisions: one is what the piece is in, the
other is what this stretch is in, and the second is stated as a departure from the first.

Both panels can be open at once. They are about different things and a reader may well be looking at
a passage and a note in it at the same time.

## 1.4 Things I believed that turned out false

I believed the dashed outline was being drawn wrongly, because it vanished the moment the toolbox
opened. The outline was correct and the score underneath it was being thrown away: the sheet is
rebuilt whenever anything it draws from changes, the toolbox handlers were written inline so they
were a new function on every render, and the rebuild took the selection with it. Two things fixed
it. The handlers are now stable, so picking a stretch does not rebuild the sheet at all. And the
selection is read back into the new score after a rebuild the sheet genuinely had to do, which is
what keeps the outline on screen after a passage is given a key.

The lesson generalises past this task: **a callback written inline in the props of `TimeScoreView`
silently rebuilds a 2669-note score on every render.** Anything passed to it has to be stable.

I also believed a panel dragged by its title bar could listen for movement on the bar itself. It
cannot: the pointer leaves the bar as soon as it moves faster than the panel follows. The listeners
are on the window, and they accept both pointer and mouse events, because not every way of driving
a page sends the first kind.
