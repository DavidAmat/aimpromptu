# The level of this run: easy

The pianist reads the melody in the right hand and simple chords in the left hand, and both hands almost never move.

## The right hand at easy

- The **main melody in single notes**: one note at a time, no chords, no octavas. Where the transcription has a chord
  or an octava in the right hand, keep its melody note (usually the top) and nothing else.
- Keep every note that you think is part of the melody. You may simplify a short melody note (write it with a
  longer or shorter figure, so that the rhythm reads simply), but you may not leave it out.
- Accompaniment chords that the transcription put in the right hand (wrong hands) move to the left hand when they
  fit its pattern; when the left hand already plays that chord, leave them out.
- No decoration notes: a short note before a melody note gets a figure of its own (a semicorchea or longer) when it
  is part of the melody, or goes (declared) when it is not.
- The checker allows one right-hand note per column, and no figure shorter than a semicorchea for a new note.

## The left hand at easy

- **Simple chords**: triads that are symmetric and in close position, such as Fa-La-Do (F3+A3+C4), major or minor
  (Fa-Lab-Do, F3+Ab3+C4), or two notes of the triad (Fa-Do, La-Do). No harder chords such as Do-Mi-Sol-Sib
  (C+E+G+Bb): play a seventh chord as its triad.
- A **low note and then the chord** is allowed: Fa2 first, then Fa3-La3-Do4 (F2 then F3+A3+C4). The low note is the
  bass of the chord, as near to the chord as the song's bass allows (an octava below its lowest note, or less). That
  jump is the only jump of the easy left hand: every chord or figure after the low note stays in one position.
- When the melody climbs or falls, the chords may move with it in steps (Fa-La-Do, Sol-Sib-Re, La-Do-Mi): the hand
  keeps one shape and slides.
- Between two chords, choose the position of the new chord nearest to the old one (an inversion, Mi-La-Do after
  Mi-Sol-Do), not a jump to the same shape higher or lower.
- Few notes: the song's rhythm is given by the pattern of the run, not by every onset of the song's left hand. A
  chord changes when the harmony changes, not more often.
- The checker allows at most 3 left-hand notes in a column, within 9 semitones (a triad); the low note and its
  chord together within 24 semitones inside two negras; and at most 0.75 moves of the hand per negra (a low note then a chord held for the rest of the chord is about 0.5).
